# Market Research: Self-Serve Home Buying Without a Buyer's Agent

_Researcher report — prepared 2026-06-06. Focus: US home buyers purchasing without a buyer's (buy-side) agent to save on commission._

---

## 1. The End-to-End US Home-Buying Journey

Overall timeline: roughly **3–6 months** from financial prep to closing. Once an offer is accepted ("under contract"), closing typically takes **30–45 days** (cash deals can be faster; some run to 60+). Sources: [Better](https://better.com/content/home-buying-process-timeline), [Redfin 17-step guide](https://www.redfin.com/blog/home-buying-process-timeline/), [AmeriSave checklist](https://www.amerisave.com/learn/your-complete-home-buying-checklist-for-essential-steps-from-preapproval-to-closing-day).

| # | Step | What happens | Typical timing | Normally involved | What the buyer must do themselves if agent-free |
|---|------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Financial prep & budgeting** | Check credit, save down payment + closing costs, set a realistic price range. 2025–26 median down payment ~10% (highest since 1989); avg rate ~6.2%. | Weeks–months before search | Buyer, sometimes a financial advisor | Run affordability math, budget for closing costs (2–5% of loan), build reserves |
| 2 | **Mortgage pre-approval** | Lender verifies income/assets/credit and issues a pre-approval letter establishing price range and credibility. | 1–3 days (shop lenders over 1–2 weeks) | Buyer, loan officer/lender | Compare multiple lenders directly; gather pay stubs, W-2s, bank statements |
| 3 | **Home search** | Browse listings (MLS via Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com), set criteria/alerts, shortlist. | ~10 weeks on average | Buyer, (normally buyer's agent) | Find listings, research neighborhoods/comps, contact listing agents directly to request showings |
| 4 | **Touring / evaluating** | Visit homes, open houses, assess condition and value vs. comps. | Ongoing during search | Buyer, listing agent | Schedule tours directly with listing agents/sellers; evaluate condition; pull own comparable sales |
| 5 | **Making an offer** | Submit a written offer: price, contingencies, earnest money, timeline, financing terms. | 1–3 days after deciding | Buyer, (agent), seller/listing agent | Draft/complete the offer (state form or attorney-drafted), set contingencies, decide earnest money amount |
| 6 | **Negotiation** | Counteroffers on price, repairs, closing costs, **and now commission/concessions**. | Days | Buyer, seller, agents | Negotiate directly with the listing agent/seller — a structural disadvantage vs. a pro on the other side |
| 7 | **Purchase agreement (under contract)** | Both parties sign a binding contract; deal goes "pending." | Same day as acceptance | Buyer, seller, (attorney) | Ensure contract terms/contingencies protect you — strongly advised to have an attorney review |
| 8 | **Earnest money deposit** | Buyer wires a good-faith deposit (typically 1–3% of price) into escrow; applied to purchase at closing, at risk if buyer defaults outside contingencies. | Within 1–3 days of signing | Buyer, escrow/title/attorney | Wire to the correct escrow holder (wire-fraud risk); confirm refund conditions |
| 9 | **Home inspection** | Licensed inspector checks structure, roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC; basis for repair/credit requests or walking away (if inspection contingency). | First 7–14 days under contract | Buyer, inspector | Hire and attend inspection; interpret report; decide on repair negotiations/credits |
| 10 | **Appraisal** | Lender orders an independent appraisal to confirm value supports the loan. Low appraisal can require renegotiation or more cash. | ~1–2 weeks after contract | Lender, appraiser, buyer pays | Track the order via lender; respond if value comes in low |
| 11 | **Title search & escrow / title insurance** | Title company/attorney searches for liens/ownership defects; opens escrow; buyer purchases lender's + (optional but recommended) owner's title insurance. | Runs in parallel, ~2–3 weeks | Title/escrow company or closing attorney | Select/coordinate the title or escrow company (or attorney in attorney states); review title commitment |
| 12 | **Financing / underwriting** | Full loan application; underwriter verifies everything, may issue conditions; ends in "clear to close." | 30–45 days | Lender, underwriter, buyer | Respond promptly to document/condition requests; avoid new credit/job changes |
| 13 | **Closing Disclosure (3-day rule)** | Lender must deliver the CD at least 3 business days before closing; buyer compares final costs to the Loan Estimate. | 3+ days before closing | Lender, buyer | Read the CD line-by-line and dispute errors |
| 14 | **Final walkthrough** | Buyer verifies condition unchanged and agreed repairs done. | 24–48 hrs before closing | Buyer, (agent) | Conduct the walkthrough yourself; flag issues before signing |
| 15 | **Closing** | Sign loan + transfer docs, pay closing costs + down payment, funds disburse, deed records, keys handed over. | 1–2 hrs (funding sometimes next day) | Buyer, seller, title/escrow or attorney, notary, lender | Attend, bring certified funds/wire, sign; an attorney can attend with you |
| 16 | **Post-purchase** | Record deed, set up utilities, file homestead exemption (where applicable), keep closing docs, start mortgage payments. | After closing | Buyer | Handle move-in, tax/insurance setup, ongoing payments |

## 2. Buying Without a Buyer's Agent

**Is it legal/possible?** Yes — it is legal in **all 50 states** to buy a home without a buyer's agent ("unrepresented buyer"). Some states require an attorney at closing (see §4), but none require a buyer's agent. ([Redfin unrepresented-buyer guide](https://www.redfin.com/blog/how-to-buy-a-home-unrepresented/), [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/should-you-buy-home-without-real-estate-agent/))

**How buyer-agent commission works (and the 2024 NAR settlement):**
- The **NAR antitrust settlement** ($418M, effective **August 17, 2024**) made two structural changes: (1) buyer-agent compensation **can no longer be advertised on the MLS**, and (2) buyers must sign a **written buyer-agency agreement before touring** homes with an agent. ([NAR FAQs](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs), [Summary of 2024 MLS changes](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/summary-of-2024-mls-changes))
- Commission is now **negotiated deal by deal** — "sellers always pay" is no longer the default. In practice many sellers still offer to cover the buyer-side commission as a concession because, in a buyer-favorable 2025 market, buyers can walk. ([onepercentlistings](https://www.onepercentlistings.com/post/how-real-estate-commission-works-in-2025), [realestatenews.com](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/08/16/after-a-year-of-nars-new-rules-commissions-are-up))
- **Reality check:** commissions did **not** fall as predicted. One year later, buyer-agent commission averaged **~2.4–2.67%** (Q1–Q2 2025), back near pre-settlement levels. On a ~$368K median home, total agent fees ~$20K split roughly evenly between the two sides. ([Redfin Q2 2025](https://www.redfin.com/news/commissions-q2-2025/), [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-agent-commissions-q2-2025-post-nar-settlement/), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/agent-commissions-edge-higher-in-2025-one-year-after-landmark-nar-settlement-302483289.html))

**How much can a buyer realistically save?** The buyer-side commission is roughly **2.5% of price** (~$7,500 on a $300K home; ~$9,800 on a $368K home). BUT savings are **not automatic**:
- If the seller offers a buyer-agent concession and the buyer is unrepresented, declining it often means the **seller keeps it**, not the buyer — unless the buyer negotiates a price reduction or has the concession credited to them. The new negotiability post-settlement is exactly what makes capturing this savings more feasible than before. ([Better](https://better.com/content/buying-house-without-realtor), [FastExpert](https://www.fastexpert.com/blog/risks-of-buying-a-house-without-a-realtor/))
- **The product opportunity:** guiding the buyer to explicitly negotiate that the unpaid buyer-side commission becomes a price cut or closing-cost credit is where real dollars land.

**Risks of going agent-free & mitigations:**
| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Missing disclosure red flags, overpaying (no comps expertise) | Tool-provided comps + disclosure checklists; require inspection |
| Mishandling contingency deadlines / contract terms | **Real estate attorney** (flat fee ~$500–$1,500+) to draft/review contract — addresses most of the agentless risk directly |
| Negotiating against a skilled listing agent | Scripts/playbooks; structured offer templates |
| Wire fraud on earnest money / closing funds | Verify wire instructions by phone with known number |
| Access/showings (some listing agents prefer represented buyers) | Use flat-fee/limited-service brokers; request showings directly |

**Hybrid/flat-fee alternatives** (middle ground between full agent and fully solo): flat-fee buyer brokers (e.g. a flat $7,995–$9,999, rebating most commission), buyer-rebate services, and **attorney-broker** combos that cap fees (e.g. ~$15K) and rebate the rest toward the purchase price. Commission rebates are legal in ~40 states. ([Flat Fee Buyers](https://www.flatfeebuyers.com/), [NPR on flat-fee brokers](https://www.npr.org/2025/05/21/nx-s1-5388943/real-estate-broker-fee-change), [Clever](https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/buyers-agent-fee/))

## 3. Key Documents & Terms — Glossary

Sources: [Nolo glossary](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/home-buying-lingo-36137.html), [Better glossary](https://better.com/glossary), [BofA terms](https://bettermoneyhabits.bankofamerica.com/en/home-ownership/5-stage-guide-to-homebuying-terms).

- **Pre-approval** — Lender's conditional commitment to lend a stated amount; sets your budget and signals seriousness.
- **Purchase agreement (PSA / sales contract)** — Binding contract between buyer and seller stating price, contingencies, and closing date.
- **Contingency** — A condition that lets a party modify or exit the contract if not met (common: inspection, appraisal, financing, sale-of-home, title).
- **Earnest money** — Good-faith deposit (1–3%) held in escrow; applied to price at closing; may be forfeited if buyer defaults outside contingencies.
- **Escrow** — Neutral third party holding funds/documents until closing conditions are met.
- **Title search** — Examination of public records for liens/ownership defects.
- **Title insurance** — Protects against title defects: **lender's policy** (required by lender) and **owner's policy** (optional but recommended; one-time premium).
- **Appraisal** — Lender-ordered independent valuation; protects the loan, not the buyer.
- **Loan Estimate (LE)** — Standardized estimate of loan terms/costs given ~3 days after application.
- **Closing Disclosure (CD)** — Final accounting of all loan terms and closing costs; must be delivered **≥3 business days before closing** for review against the LE.
- **Clear to close** — Underwriting fully approved; ready to schedule closing.
- **Final walkthrough** — Buyer's last condition check 24–48 hrs before closing.
- **Closing / settlement** — Signing, fund disbursement, deed recording, key handover.
- **Seller disclosure** — Seller's statement of known property defects (scope varies sharply by state — see §4).
- **Concession** — Credit from seller (e.g., toward closing costs or, post-settlement, buyer-side commission).

## 4. State-by-State Variation (High Level)

Sources: [retipster closing-agent map](https://retipster.com/real-estate-closing-agents/), [Notary Training School 50-state list](https://notarytrainingschool.com/a-notary-signing-agents-guide-to-attorney-states-and-escrow-states/), [Real Estate Law Corp on disclosures](https://www.realestatelawcorp.com/seller-disclosure-requirements-by-state-variations-and-key-considerations/).

- **Attorney states** (lawyer must conduct/oversee closing): e.g. **GA, SC, NC, CT, MA, NY, NJ, DE, RI, NH, ME, VT, WV, ND, parts of others**. Lists vary by source and some states are "attorney-influenced." For a self-serve buyer here, an attorney is already mandatory — bundle it.
- **Escrow / title-company states** (title or escrow company can close without an attorney): e.g. **AZ, CA, CO, NV, OR, WA, TX, UT, ID, OH, MO, MN, WI, IN, MI, KS, NE, NM, AK, MT, OK, SD, ND, IL, MD**.
- **Disclosure regimes differ widely:** California is among the most stringent (mandatory Transfer Disclosure Statement under Civ. Code §1102 et seq.); other states use "caveat emptor"-leaning rules where sellers disclose far less. A self-serve buyer must know their state's required disclosure forms.
- **Implication:** the product needs a **state layer** — driving closing flow (attorney vs. escrow), required disclosure forms, and standard contract templates per state.

## 5. Competitor / Existing-Tools Landscape

Sources: [HomeLight Redfin vs Zillow](https://www.homelight.com/blog/redfin-vs-zillow/), [Houzeo](https://www.houzeo.com/blog/redfin-vs-zillow/), [anytimeestimate Flyhomes](https://anytimeestimate.com/reviews/flyhomes/), [Houwzer reviews](https://www.realestatewitch.com/houwzer-reviews/), [HowStuffWorks tech tools](https://home.howstuffworks.com/real-estate/buying-home/10-tech-tools-when-buying-a-house.htm).

| Player | What it does | Relation to no-agent buying |
|--------|--------------|-----------------------------|
| **Zillow** | Listings "Super App" + Zestimate, Zillow Home Loans, ShowingTime; routes you to agents. Does **not** employ agents to represent buyers. | Discovery only; pushes you toward an agent, not away from one |
| **Redfin** (now "Powered by Rocket," acquired July 2025 for $1.75B) | Full-stack brokerage with W-2 lead agents; rich buyer guides. | Still an agent model; some buyer rebates |
| **Houwzer** | 1% listing fee + buyer rebate (up to half of commission above 1%); ~18 states + DC. | Discount brokerage, still agent-mediated |
| **Flyhomes** | Cash-offer / buy-before-you-sell financing; charges loan origination + commission + closing. | Financing edge, not agent-free guidance |
| **Flat-fee buyer brokers / rebate services** (Flat Fee Buyers, ShopProp, attorney-brokers) | Flat fee, rebate bulk of buyer commission. | Closest to the savings thesis, but still broker-fronted, fragmented, not a guided product |
| **HUD.gov, Investopedia, lender guides** | Educational checklists/glossaries. | Static content, not transactional/guided |

**The gap:** No mainstream product is a **guided, transactional, state-aware self-serve workflow for the unrepresented buyer**. Listing portals optimize discovery and funnel users to agents (their revenue source). Discount brokers still insert an agent. Educational sites are static. **Nothing walks a buyer through offer → contract → earnest money → inspection → title/escrow → closing with state-specific templates, deadline tracking, attorney/inspector marketplace, and commission-savings negotiation guidance.** That is the white space.

## 6. Costs Breakdown

Sources: [Bankrate closing costs](https://www.bankrate.com/mortgages/what-are-closing-costs/), [Zillow closing costs](https://www.zillow.com/learn/closing-costs/).

**Buyer closing costs total ~2–5% of the loan amount** (separate from the down payment). Typical line items:

| Cost | Typical range |
|------|---------------|
| Loan origination | ~$1,000–$2,000 (varies by lender) |
| Underwriting / processing | ~$500–$900 |
| Appraisal | $500–$1,000+ |
| Title search + lender's + owner's title insurance | $300–$2,500+ |
| Settlement / escrow fee | ~1% of price ($350–$1,000+) |
| Recording fees | ~$100 |
| Prepaid property taxes + homeowners insurance (escrow setup) | $1,000–$4,500+ |
| Prepaid interest | varies (e.g. ~$1,000 for 15 days) |

**Where commission savings land:** Buyers traditionally **do not pay buyer-agent commission directly** — it came out of the seller's proceeds. Post-NAR-settlement this is **shifting and now negotiated**. A self-serve buyer's savings (~2.5% of price) only materialize if they **negotiate the unpaid buyer-side commission into a price reduction or seller credit at closing**; otherwise the seller simply pockets it. Capturing that delta is the core value proposition and must be an explicit, guided step in the product.

## Recommended Canonical Journey Stages (drives the product)

A linear, trackable pipeline. Each stage = a module with checklists, document templates, deadline tracking, and "do-it-yourself vs. bring-in-a-pro" guidance.

1. **Get Ready** — budget, credit, down-payment + closing-cost savings target
2. **Get Pre-Approved** — compare lenders, secure pre-approval letter
3. **Search** — find listings, set criteria, pull comps
4. **Tour & Evaluate** — schedule showings directly, assess condition/value
5. **Make an Offer** — state-specific offer + contingencies + earnest money + **commission-savings negotiation**
6. **Negotiate & Go Under Contract** — counteroffers, signed purchase agreement (attorney review)
7. **Earnest Money & Open Escrow** — deposit safely, open escrow/title
8. **Inspection** — schedule, interpret, negotiate repairs/credits
9. **Appraisal & Financing/Underwriting** — track lender conditions to "clear to close"
10. **Title & Escrow** — title search, choose owner's title insurance, state closing path (attorney vs. escrow)
11. **Closing Disclosure Review** — 3-day rule, verify vs. Loan Estimate
12. **Final Walkthrough**
13. **Closing / Settlement** — sign, fund, record, get keys
14. **Post-Purchase** — record-keeping, utilities, tax exemptions, payments

## Implications for Our Product

1. **We're filling genuine white space.** No incumbent offers a guided, state-aware, transactional self-serve workflow for unrepresented buyers — portals funnel to agents; discount brokers still insert an agent; educational sites are static.
2. **The savings are real but NOT automatic.** ~2.5% (~$7.5K–$10K+) only lands if the buyer negotiates the unpaid buyer-side commission into a price cut or seller credit. **Make this an explicit, scripted, high-visibility step** — it is the product's core promise.
3. **Lean into the post-NAR-settlement moment.** Aug 2024 changes (no MLS commission advertising, mandatory buyer-agency agreements, fully negotiable commission) created the structural opening and buyer awareness that makes agent-free buying timely.
4. **Mitigate the real risks by design.** Embed contract/disclosure checklists, contingency-deadline tracking, comps, and an **attorney + inspector marketplace** — an affordable flat-fee attorney neutralizes most of the agentless legal risk.
5. **Build a state layer.** Attorney-vs-escrow closing path, state disclosure forms, and standard contract templates per state are non-negotiable for trust and legality.
6. **The 14-stage canonical pipeline above should be the product's backbone**, each stage a guided module with templates, deadlines, and pro-handoff points.
7. **Guard the trust-critical moments:** wire-fraud warnings on earnest money/closing, the CD 3-day review, and final walkthrough are where self-serve buyers get burned — surface them prominently.
